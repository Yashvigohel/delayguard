The dataset

DelayGuard learns from the DataCo Smart Supply Chain data, a public dataset of orders with delivery outcomes.

Name
DataCo Smart Supply Chain for Big Data Analysis
Source
Kaggle, originally published on Mendeley Data
Citation
Constante, F., Silva, F., & Pereira, A. (2019). DataCo Smart Supply Chain for Big Data Analysis. Mendeley Data, V5. doi.org/10.17632/8gx2fvg2k6.5
Licence
Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0)
Original size
180,519 orders, 53 columns
Working sample
45,000 orders, stratified on the target. 31 columns after cleaning, 60 model inputs after preparation.
Target
Late_delivery_risk. 1 means late (54.8% of the sample), 0 means on time (45.2%).
Scheduled days
Fixed by shipping mode in this data: Same Day 0, First Class 1, Second Class 2, Standard Class 4.

What the exploration showed

  • Shipping mode is the strongest signal. First Class orders were late 95.5% of the time and Standard Class 37.6% (9,000 test orders).
  • Same Day orders follow a time rule. Orders placed before 12:00 were never late, and orders placed from 12:00 on were late in 89% or more of cases for every hour.
  • Order region barely separates late from on-time orders. Product category varies more, with Strength Training the highest among those plotted.
  • Some orders lose a lot of money (as low as -4,274.98 per order). They look like real losses, not errors, so they were kept.

What was removed, and why

  • Personal details and IDs, because they identify people or rows and say nothing about delay.
  • Real shipping days and delivery status, because they are only known after delivery.
  • Order status, because it describes how the order ended.
  • City, state, country and category-name text, because each has far too many values for a simple encoding.
