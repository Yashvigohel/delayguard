How it works

From raw orders to a live prediction, in the order the work was done.

  1. Start from the DataCo orders

    The original file has 180,519 orders and 53 columns, with a flag showing whether each order was delivered late.

  2. Remove what cannot be known at order time

    22 columns were dropped: personal details, ID numbers, duplicate, empty and constant columns, and two that reveal the outcome (the real shipping days and the delivery status).

  3. Sample and split

    A stratified sample of 45,000 orders keeps the late and on-time shares unchanged. 36,000 orders train the models and 9,000 stay untouched until the final test.

  4. Prepare the inputs

    Order dates become month, weekday and hour. Six very detailed text columns (cities, states, countries and category name) were dropped, the rest were one-hot encoded, and numbers were scaled using the training data only.

  5. Check for leakage again

    Order status looked harmless but describes how an order ended. Removing it lowered ROC-AUC from 0.809 to 0.781 in cross-validation. We accepted the lower score because it is the honest one.

  6. Train and tune

    A baseline that always says late, Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest and XGBoost were compared with 5-fold cross-validation. Random Forest and XGBoost were tuned with randomized search on ROC-AUC.

  7. Test once and choose

    On the test orders, Random Forest and XGBoost tied (ROC-AUC 0.790 and 0.789). XGBoost was chosen because its saved file is 2.0 MB against 23.8 MB, with the same recall.

  8. Serve it

    A FastAPI service returns the late probability, a high or low risk decision at a cut-off of 0.385 that was set on training data, and the top three SHAP factors.
